# Internal pitch preparation — not sent

## Positioning

An independent football research and publishing prototype. Demonstrate how an editor can turn historical results into a scoped, inspectable statistic and an exportable graphic. Do not imply affiliation with The Touchline or that the working product name establishes a partnership.

## Three-minute demonstration

1. Open Research studio with Barcelona and Real Madrid. The bundled records give 27 wins, 14 draws and 20 defeats for Barcelona across 61 recorded La Liga meetings, September 30, 1995–October 26, 2025. The selected scope is visible beside the claim.
2. Inspect supporting source rows. Explain that the evidence fingerprint detects a change in the result set; it does not independently certify the original data.
3. Switch from the W/D/L card to goal difference or recorded win rate. Change the byline and graphic format. Export a PNG, caption and evidence JSON. Publication remains subject to editorial review and documented data rights.
4. Show the cross-era attack index: club goals per match divided by the league scoring environment in the same available season. Explain partial seasons and the absence of opponent-strength adjustment.
5. Show historical data checks and the evaluated forecast lab. Make the boundary explicit: historical demo works today; live subscriptions and hosting are separate setup requirements.

## Pilot hypothesis

Editors may save research and production time while gaining inspectable evidence. This is an unvalidated hypothesis. Ask the team to describe its actual research process, frequently needed comparisons, sources, brand templates and publishing requirements before committing to additional features.

Suggest a bounded evaluation with a small set of editors and agreed tasks. Measure task completion time, errors found during review, graphics actually used, and editor feedback. Audience clicks or engagement are optional later measures; do not promise growth or revenue.

## Draft introduction

I built an independent football analytics prototype and have been developing an editorial research workflow around it. It lets an editor investigate historical match results, inspect the evidence behind a statistic, and export a graphic and caption with the coverage clearly attached. I would welcome the chance to show you a short demonstration and learn whether a small pilot could support your team's work.

## Questions to learn from the team

Which comparisons take the most research time? Which data sources are already licensed? Who reviews statistical claims? What output sizes and templates fit the publishing workflow? What would make a pilot useful enough to continue?
